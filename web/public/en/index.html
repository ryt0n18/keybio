Keystroke dynamics biometrics

It knows you by the way you type.

KeyBio measures how long you hold down each key, how long you take to move on to the next one and which key combinations slow you down. That is enough to tell one person from another, with no passwords or cameras.

How it works

It's not what you type, it's how

The way you type is as personal as your handwriting. KeyBio turns it into a fingerprint and compares it with those of the people who have enrolled.

  1. 1. Capture

    It records the moment you press and release each key. It does not keep a recording of what you type to read it: it is only interested in the timing.

  2. 2. Features

    It calculates more than twenty features: how long you hold each key, flight time, the speed of each pair and trio of letters, how you use capital letters and how you correct mistakes.

  3. 3. Comparison

    It compares your sample with each fingerprint: which combinations are your fastest and slowest (R measure), how many take the same time (A measure) and how far apart your overall features are.

  4. 4. Personal threshold

    Each person has their own threshold, calculated by comparing their samples with each other. If no fingerprint is similar enough, the answer is “not enrolled”.

What the program includes

  • Identification (who am I?) and verification (am I this person?), also with free text.
  • Live estimate while you type and a feature-by-feature explanation of the result.
  • Profiles that adapt with use and automatic calibration with your own data.
  • Impersonation detection: automated typing and replayed recordings.
  • Dashboard, history, heat maps and PDF reports.
  • Password protection and backups.

Your data is yours

The program keeps everything on your own computer, optionally encrypted with a password, and sends nothing over the internet. Neither does the demo on this page: everything is calculated in your browser.

How far it goes

In a test with 20 simulated people (computer-generated typing) and 30 strangers, on “normal” sensitivity, it recognised the right person in 79 out of 80 attempts and rejected 29 of the 30 strangers. This is simulated data: with real people the results may be worse, so take it as a guide, not a guarantee.
